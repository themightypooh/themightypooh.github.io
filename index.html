Geppetto

Editor tools for s&box: model, rig and animate without leaving the engine.

Effigy: a parametric modeller

  • Five workspaces: CAD with an editable feature tree, Model (edit, sculpt, retopo), Paint, Rig and Clothing. Exports a real .vmdl.
  • Full edit-mode toolkit: extrude, inset, bevel, loop cut, knife, bridge, live modifiers, UVs and weight painting.
  • Clothing is built from the body's bones, so it refits when the body changes. Also exports live cloth.
  • The .effigy format is plain text with one line per feature, so it diffs cleanly in git.
  • The modelling kernel has no engine dependency. It runs headlessly from C#, build scripts or a CLI, backed by a large test suite.

Marionette: an animation editor

  • Pose bones, keyframe with easing curves and auto-key, then bake clips or play them in game.
  • Two-bone IK, joint limits, and prop collision while posing.
  • Fits a custom model to the player skeleton and writes a walkable test scene in one click.

s&box · WIP

Survey Sentence

A prisoner, sealed alone in a windowless pod, finds and prints subjects using a sonar scope and a 3D printer. It's a PS1-style remake of my earlier game Deep Survey, which you can play now.

  • The sonar scope turns an outside camera into echolocation through a depth post-process, displayed on a curved CRT.
  • A true first-person body, with animation takes checked for how fast they swing the camera.
  • A layered sound director with ducking and loudness (LUFS) targets.

s&box · WIP

Inside The Arcade

A first-person retro arcade where every cabinet runs its own playable game, and many let you go inside them.

  • Climb into the games. Win the MIND-LINK headset at the prize counter and GO INSIDE puts you in the machine: the sub in Torpedo Alley, the bass boat in Lunker Lake, the kitchen in Cover Fire.
  • See through the cabinets. Win the X-ray specs and look straight through any machine's shell at the boards, motors, CRT and wiring inside, with markers that explain each part.
  • 28 real games, not props. A kitchen brawler, a rally stage, a shmup, a zombie light-gun holdout, skee-ball and a physics claw machine.
  • Four pinball tables on custom physics, each in its own era of cabinet, with dot-matrix and video displays.
  • A few hours after school. Feed tokens, win tickets, trade them at the prize counter, and be at the doors when Mom comes to pick you up.
  • Every kid on the floor is generated: one low-poly body, one 256 px atlas, thousands of clip-checked looks.
  • Each game is tested headlessly with simulations and soak runs, and the packed floor holds 60 fps.

Pinoch

An AI I built completely from scratch that learns to talk the way a kid does, starting from nothing. Pinoch is its body, and he grows up as it learns. This is what he looks like before he's been given anything: no lessons and not even his first words.

This is the real viewer on a two-minute demo run; on my desktop it follows the live training and I can talk to him. Tap to drop, hold to stream, pick a colour up top, and click Pinoch to pet him or right-click to poke.

NPC brain

How a model like this drives a game NPC. Vision and hearing feed Kev, a small fast classifier: obvious stuff (a grenade beep, "jump") becomes an action straight away, like a reflex. Anything that needs thought goes to the language model with mission and memory, and comes back out as speech.
